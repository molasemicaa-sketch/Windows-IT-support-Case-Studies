## Case Study 10: Full Storage

## Problem Statement

An employee reports that their Windows computer is running out of storage space. When attempting to save files, install applications, or download documents, Windows displays a warning that the disk is almost full. The computer may also become slow, updates may fail to install, and applications may not function properly. The employee needs sufficient storage space to continue working efficiently and complete an important work assignment.

## Initial Questions

1. What error message appears when the employee attempts to save files or install applications?

2. How much free storage space is available on the computer's system drive?

3. Has the employee recently downloaded large files, installed new applications, or saved many documents and videos?

4. Is the storage problem affecting only the system drive, or are other drives and connected storage devices also full?

## Possible Causes

- Accumulation of temporary files and cached data.

- Large files, such as videos, downloads, and archived documents, consuming disk space.

- Too many installed applications or games.

- Recycle Bin containing deleted files that have not been permanently removed.

- Large backup files, old Windows installation files, or previous update files.

- Cloud synchronization settings storing more files locally than expected.

### Troubleshooting Process

1. **Check available storage space.** Open **Settings > System > Storage** and review the amount of used and available space on the system drive. Identify which categories, such as installed applications, temporary files, or documents, are consuming the most storage.

2. **Identify large files and folders.** Open File Explorer and inspect common locations such as Downloads, Videos, Documents, and Desktop. Sort files by size to identify large items that may no longer be needed. Confirm ownership and business requirements before moving or deleting company files.

3. **Remove temporary files.** Open Windows Storage settings and select **Temporary files**. Review the listed categories carefully and remove only files that are safe to delete. Avoid selecting Downloads or other categories containing important data unless their contents have been verified.

4. **Review the Recycle Bin.** Check the Recycle Bin for files that are no longer needed. Confirm that important files have been backed up or are safe to remove before emptying it, because permanently deleted files may be difficult to recover.

5. **Uninstall unnecessary applications.** Open **Settings > Apps > Installed apps** and review installed software. Uninstall applications that are no longer needed and that the employee is authorized to remove. Do not uninstall required company software or security tools.

6. **Check Windows update and backup files.** Review whether old update files, temporary installation files, or local backups are consuming significant space. Use Windows Storage settings or approved cleanup tools. Avoid manually deleting system folders or backup files that may be required for recovery.

7. **Analyze the results.** Recheck the available storage space after each cleanup step. If the drive remains nearly full, investigate whether business data should be moved to approved network storage or cloud storage. If storage usage increases unexpectedly, check for unusually large logs, repeated downloads, or applications generating excessive data.

### Resolution

For this example, suppose the investigation identifies accumulated temporary files and large, unnecessary downloads as the main causes of the full storage problem.

The technician opens Windows Storage settings and reviews the temporary files listed by the system. After confirming that the selected files are safe to remove, the technician deletes the unnecessary temporary data. The technician then reviews the Downloads folder with the employee and removes obsolete files that are no longer required for business operations.

Important work documents are retained, and files that need long-term storage are moved to an approved company storage location.

The technician checks the system drive again and confirms that sufficient storage space has been recovered. The employee successfully saves new documents, and Windows has enough available space to continue normal operations.

### Preventive Measures

- Monitor available disk space regularly through Windows Storage settings.

- Remove unnecessary temporary files and obsolete downloads periodically.

- Store work documents in approved locations and avoid keeping unnecessary duplicate copies.

- Use approved network or cloud storage for large files that do not need to remain on the computer.

- Review installed applications and uninstall unnecessary software when authorized.

- Configure storage management features, such as Storage Sense, according to company policy.

- Maintain appropriate backups before deleting or moving important files.

- Report recurring storage problems to IT support, especially when the drive fills up unexpectedly or may be experiencing a hardware issue.

### Conclusion

The case demonstrates a systematic approach to troubleshooting a full-storage problem. By checking available disk space, identifying large files, removing unnecessary temporary data, reviewing installed applications, and managing stored documents carefully, a technician can recover storage space and restore normal computer operation. Regular storage monitoring, responsible file management, and approved backup practices help prevent storage-related interruptions and maintain employee productivity.
