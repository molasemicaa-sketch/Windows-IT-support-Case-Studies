## Case Study 08: Suspicious Email or Possible Phishing

### Problem Statement

An employee reports receiving a suspicious email that appears to come from a trusted organization, such as a bank, company department, or online service. The email asks the employee to click a link, open an attachment, or provide sensitive information, such as login credentials. The employee is unsure whether the message is legitimate and is concerned that interacting with it could compromise their account or the company's network.

### Initial Questions

1. Who is the sender, and does the email address match the official address of the organization it claims to represent?

2. Does the email contain suspicious links, unexpected attachments, urgent requests, or unusual spelling and grammar?

3. Has the employee clicked a link, downloaded an attachment, entered credentials, or replied to the email?

4. Was the email received by other employees, and does it appear to be related to a legitimate business activity?

### Possible Causes

- A phishing email designed to steal usernames, passwords, or other sensitive information.

- An email containing malicious attachments that may install malware.

- A spoofed sender address that imitates a legitimate person or organization.

- A fraudulent message that creates urgency to pressure the recipient into taking action.

- A compromised legitimate email account being used to send malicious messages.

### Troubleshooting Process

1. **Do not interact with the suspicious email.** Advise the employee not to click links, open attachments, reply to the sender, or provide sensitive information. If the email is still open, avoid interacting with its contents.

2. **Inspect the sender's information.** Check the sender's full email address and compare the domain with the organization's official domain. Be aware that a familiar display name does not guarantee that an email is legitimate.

3. **Examine the message content.** Look for unexpected requests, unusual payment instructions, threats of account suspension, urgent demands, suspicious links, or unexpected attachments. Remember that professional-looking emails can also be fraudulent.

4. **Verify the request independently.** If the message claims to come from a manager, bank, or service provider, contact the person or organization through a known, trusted communication channel. Do not use the contact details or links provided in the suspicious email.

5. **Report the email to IT support.** Use the organization's approved phishing-reporting button or reporting procedure. Preserve the message and its relevant details so the security team can investigate. Avoid forwarding it to coworkers unless instructed to do so by the security team.

6. **Determine whether the employee interacted with the email.** Ask whether the employee clicked a link, opened an attachment, entered a password, or downloaded a file. If any interaction occurred, record what happened and when, without delaying escalation to IT support.

7. **Apply the appropriate response.** If the email is confirmed or strongly suspected to be malicious, the security team can investigate the message, block malicious senders or domains where appropriate, and remove similar messages from other mailboxes. If credentials were entered, reset the affected password through an approved process and review account activity. If an attachment was executed or a suspicious file was downloaded, follow the organization's incident response procedures and isolate the affected device from the network if instructed or authorized.

### Resolution

For this example, suppose the investigation identifies the email as a phishing attempt designed to steal the employee's company login credentials.

The employee reports the message to IT support without clicking its links or opening its attachments. The security team examines the sender's information, message content, and embedded link and confirms that the email is fraudulent.

The team then follows the organization's security procedures to block the malicious sender or link where appropriate and searches for similar messages received by other employees. The suspicious email is removed or quarantined using approved security tools.

The technician informs the employee that the message was a phishing attempt and explains how to recognize similar threats. Since the employee did not interact with the email, no credential reset or device remediation is required based on this incident alone.

The employee confirms that the message has been reported and resumes work safely.

### Preventive Measures

- Provide regular cybersecurity awareness training to help employees recognize phishing attempts.

- Verify unusual requests for passwords, payments, confidential information, or urgent actions through trusted communication channels.

- Avoid clicking unexpected links or opening unexpected attachments.

- Use multifactor authentication and strong, unique passwords for company accounts.

- Keep email security tools, Windows, browsers, and antivirus software updated according to company policy.

- Report suspicious emails promptly using the organization's approved reporting procedure.

- Never share passwords, authentication codes, or confidential company information through unverified channels.

- Conduct regular security awareness exercises and communicate new phishing threats to employees.

### Conclusion

The case demonstrates a systematic approach to handling suspicious emails and possible phishing attacks. By avoiding interaction with suspicious content, examining sender information, verifying requests independently, reporting the message, and following the appropriate incident response procedures, employees and IT technicians can reduce the risk of credential theft, malware infection, and data breaches. Regular cybersecurity awareness and prompt reporting help protect company systems, sensitive information, and workplace productivity.
